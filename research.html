Research

Turning lupus genetics into biology

Our genome scans have helped pinpoint chromosomal regions harbouring lupus susceptibility genes. Together with collaborators, we have helped map more than 180 lupus risk loci. The challenge now is to turn those statistical signals into mechanisms.

Our approach

From association to mechanism

Most lupus risk variants lie outside genes, in the DNA that switches genes on and off. We follow each signal through four steps.

Map

Find risk loci in families and case-control cohorts from many ancestries.

GWASLinkageAdmixture mapping

Fine-map

Combine cohorts and condition on lead signals to narrow down the variants behind each association.

Meta-analysisConditional analysisImputation

Prioritise

Use epigenomic marks, eQTLs and chromatin interactions to rank the variants most likely to be functional.

Validate

Test allele-specific activity in immune cells and edit the variant to see its effect.

Theme 1

From variant to mechanism

Post-GWAS functional genomics: following a non-coding risk variant to the genes it controls and the immune pathway it changes.

Theme 2

Lupus risk across ancestries

Lupus is more common and more severe in people of non-European ancestry. We study diverse populations to find risk loci and to see which effects are shared across ancestries and which are not.

Our toolkit

Models and methods

We model disease-relevant processes in vitro and in vivo using patient-derived immune cells, primary tissues, cell lines and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs).

Our goal is to link genetic variation to disease phenotypes and identify new therapeutic targets and reliable biomarkers.

  • High-throughput omics: transcriptomics, epigenomics and chromatin accessibility
  • 3D chromatin organisation: 3C, Hi-C and ChIP-seq to map long-range regulatory interactions
  • CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing: introduce or correct risk variants and measure their effects
  • Genetic epidemiology: linkage, GWAS, admixture mapping and meta-analysis
